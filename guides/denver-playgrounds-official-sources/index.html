Denver Playgrounds: 5 Official City Sources to Check

Denver's park pages are useful, but they do not always answer the same practical questions. This guide pulls together five playground and play-space records where the city documents a completed project, a current project schedule, or specific play features. Anything the source does not confirm stays unknown.

This is not a complete list of Denver playgrounds, and the places have not been personally tested. The goal is simpler: give families a short, source-backed starting point and make the gaps obvious before anyone gets in the car.

1. Sloan's Lake South Playground

Denver marks the South Playground and tennis-court replacement project complete as of October 2023. The city also records a useful correction to older plans: permitting issues removed the proposed splash pad, and that area became an imaginative sand-play space instead.

Practical details: Exact street address: unknown on this project page · Parking: unknown · Current restroom operation: unknown · Accessibility details: unknown · Site-specific hours: unknown

Before you go: Treat any older image or concept that shows a splash pad as obsolete.

Source: Sloan's Lake Neighborhood Projects, City and County of Denver

2. Rosamond Park

Location: 8051 E. Quincy Avenue, Denver, CO 80237.

Rosamond is a 35-acre community park in the Hampden South neighborhood. Denver says replacement of the existing playground and basketball court was completed in spring 2024, and its April 25, 2024 ribbon-cutting record identifies the new playground at this address.

Practical details: Play equipment: unknown · Water feature: unknown · Parking: unknown · Current restroom operation: unknown · Accessibility details: unknown

Source: Rosamond Park Improvements, City and County of Denver

3. Central Park Playground

Location: Off Martin Luther King Boulevard near Xenia Street.

The city describes play-surface mounds, synthetic turf, sand play, a climbing wall, play equipment, artificial rockwork with a slide, and a misting area. A 2022 project update also lists new walks, playground surfacing, shade structures, lighting, a water pump and misters, a zip line, irrigation, and plantings.

Practical details: Parking: unknown · Current restroom operation: unknown · Current mister operation and seasonal schedule: unknown · Project completion: not explicitly confirmed on the page

Before you go: The page says construction was anticipated to finish in spring 2023. That is not the same as a current opening or water-feature confirmation, so verify before planning around the misters.

Source: Central & Greenway Park Playgrounds, City and County of Denver

4. Crestmoor Park

Location: 95 S. Monaco St. Pkwy, Denver, CO 80224.

The second phase of Crestmoor improvements focuses on replacing the existing playground and improving access to the playground and other park amenities. The project schedule labels spring 2026 as complete. An earlier phase, completed in winter 2021, added an accessible picnic area and walk improvements.

Practical details: Current playground opening: unknown · Exact accessibility features: unknown · Parking: unknown · Water feature: unknown · Current restroom operation: unknown

Before you go: The page does not include a separate post-construction reopening announcement.

Source: Crestmoor Park, City and County of Denver

5. Kentucky & Knox Park

Location: Westwood neighborhood; the project page does not state a numbered address.

Denver describes this pocket-park redesign as a new playground, irrigation system, and walk connections around the playground and picnic shelter. Its displayed schedule labels fall 2024 as project complete.

Practical details: Exact address: unknown · Current opening: unknown · Equipment: unknown · Restroom: unknown · Parking: unknown · Accessibility details: unknown · Water feature: unknown

Before you go: Do not confuse Kentucky & Knox with the separately documented Kentucky & Irving Pocket Park on the same neighborhood-project page.

Source: Westwood Neighborhood Projects, City and County of Denver

Denver park hours and restroom caveats

Denver Park Rangers lists general urban-park hours as 5 a.m. to 11 p.m. No park access is allowed when closed except on through roads or trails. A project page may have a more specific access condition, and temporary closures can override the general schedule.

Denver's Park Restrooms dataset describes permanent restroom facilities, but its page says it was last updated June 16, 2024. A dataset record does not prove that a restroom is currently unlocked, operational, or available year-round.

Official references: Denver Park Rangers and Denver Park Restrooms dataset

How these places were selected

Every entry has a current City and County of Denver page that explicitly discusses a playground or play-space project. Project schedules are reported as the city displays them, without turning a planned date into an independently verified reopening. Parking, current restroom operation, accessibility details, water-feature operation, and equipment stay unknown wherever the official page is silent.
