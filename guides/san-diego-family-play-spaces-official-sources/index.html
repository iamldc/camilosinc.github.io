San Diego Family Play Spaces: 3 Official City Sources

San Diego has beach parks, lake parks, and large regional recreation areas, but their official pages answer different planning questions. This guide compares three options where the City explicitly documents playgrounds or children's play space. Anything the source does not confirm stays unknown.

This is not a complete list, and these places have not been personally tested. The goal is a short, source-backed starting point for choosing the kind of outing that fits your day.

1. Chollas Lake Park

Address: 6350 College Grove Drive, San Diego, CA 92115.

The City lists two play options: a large playground at Gloria's Mesa, reached from a driveway west of the main parking lot, and a smaller playground on the south side of the lake with swings facing the water. The page also documents nature trails and a lake reserved for youth fishing by children ages 15 and under.

Hours: 6:30 a.m. to 30 minutes before sunset. The City says closing times are posted at each entrance and may change.

Practical details: Exact playground equipment: unknown · Playground accessibility details: unknown · Restrooms: unknown on the current page · Parking capacity: unknown · Admission cost: unknown

Before you go: Check the closing time at the gate you use. The main east gate closes 30 minutes before sunset, and other gates close earlier.

Source: Chollas Lake Park, City of San Diego

2. Kellogg Park at La Jolla Shores

The City describes Kellogg Park as a large grassy park at La Jolla Shores beach with a playground near the boardwalk, about 75 yards south of the Lifeguard Station. The City also lists several restrooms with showers at the site.

Hours: 4 a.m. to 10 p.m.; parking-lot gates close at 10 p.m.

Practical details: Playground equipment: unknown · Playground accessibility details: unknown · Ordinary park-entry cost: not stated · Food allowed, but no glass containers or alcohol · Inflatable jumps are not allowed

Before you go: The City warns that the parking lot fills quickly during summer weekends. A listed fee contact applies to permit information; the page does not state an ordinary entry fee.

Source: Kellogg Park, City of San Diego

3. Mission Bay Park

Mission Bay Park covers more than 4,235 acres of land and water. The City documents children's playgrounds, close to 14 miles of bike paths, walking paths, basketball courts, boat facilities, public restrooms and showers, and lifeguard stations in designated areas.

Practical details: Exact playground locations and equipment: use the City's linked facilities map · Playground accessibility details: unknown on the overview page · Site-specific parking: unknown · Site-specific hours: unknown · Swimming is limited to designated areas

Before you go: Treat Mission Bay as a region to narrow down, not a single destination. Check the facilities map for the exact park site and the County's current beach and bay status before planning around water access.

Source: Mission Bay Park, City of San Diego

How to choose

  • Choose Chollas Lake when you want two documented playground choices plus trails, and you can work with a sunset-based closing schedule.
  • Choose Kellogg Park when a beach-adjacent playground and on-site restrooms matter, and you can plan around crowded parking.
  • Choose Mission Bay when you want to compare several regional recreation sites and are willing to use the facilities map to pick the exact stop.
